## Business Findings

#### 1. Strong click-through rates (CTR) does not guarantee strong return

CMP_001 achieved one of the highest CTRs at 6.18%, but produced a Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) of only 3.80 USD. By comparison, CMP_023 had a lower CTR of 4.77% but generated the highest ROAS at 505.43 USD.

**Business implication**: CTR should not be used as the primary measure of campaign success. Product teams should evaluate clicks alongside conversion, attributed revenue, and ROAS


#### 2. Similar levels of engagement can product very different business outcomes

Many campaigns have CTRs clustered around 4.6%, yet their ROAS varies dramatically. For example, CMP_023 achieved a 4.77% CTR and 505.43 USD ROAS, while CMP_048 achieved a 4.13% CTR and only 0.47 USD ROAS.

**Business implication**: Click engagement explains only part of campaign performance. Revenue per conversion, bid cost, and conversion behavior need to be considered before making budget decisions.


#### 3. Sponsored-search position strongly influences customer engagement

CTR declines consistently as sponsored products move lower in search results, failing from 9.73% at position 1 to 1.94% at position 10.

**Business implication**: Higher sponsored-search placement substantially improves the likelihood of a click. However, campaign-level results show that higher engagement does not automatically produce higgher ROAS, meaning placement decisions should balance visibility with downstream conversion and revenue outcomes.
